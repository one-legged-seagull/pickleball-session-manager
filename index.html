Pickleball Session Manager:

Players

  • Put each player in a group: 1 is the strongest, then 2 and 3; B is Beginner. Use only the groups you need.
  • A new session shows Group 1 and Beginner; tap Add group to show Group 2, then Group 3 (a group with players is always shown). Rank players within their group, strongest at the top, by dragging the ⋮⋮ handle on the left of a row, or with the ▲▼ buttons. A player you move to another group joins it at the bottom; a new player starts at the bottom of their group.
  • Tick No singles on a player to keep them off the singles court and from being the lone player in a 2 v 1 (including beginners). If nobody else can fill the spot, the round shows a warning.
  • Tap Edit session name next to the title to name the session; tap the name to change it. + New (top right) starts a new session: after you confirm, it asks for a name (it suggests today's day, such as "Thursday") and then clears the players and rounds (the number of rounds and session style are kept). This cannot be undone.
  • Untick Active for an injury or departure, and tick it again for a late arrival. Then regenerate on the Fixtures tab.
  • Players already used in a round can be deactivated but not removed.

Fixtures

  • Generate rounds (it reads Regenerate rounds once there are fixtures) rebuilds every round that is not locked. It is greyed out when nothing has changed since the rounds were generated, because the same settings always give the same rounds. Change the roster, ranking, style, round count or a lock and it comes back. Each round card has icon buttons: the pencil edits the round, the padlock locks it, or unlocks it again when it is locked (a locked round shows a Locked badge, is kept exactly as it is, and its pencil is hidden until you unlock it), and the two touching paddles (the thank-you gesture) mark the round as completed: the card is greyed out with a slash through it and stays locked. Tap the ✕ on a round's Completed badge to bring it back, and the round goes back to being locked or unlocked as it was before. Press and hold or hover an icon to see its name.
  • Session style: choose Competitive, Social or Club sheets (greyed out unless there are exactly 5 rounds and 6 to 14 active players). See How the fixtures work below for the difference.
  • Use the pencil to edit a round and swap players: tap one, then tap another. Singles and 2 v 1 courts show empty seats: move a player onto one to grow that court (singles becomes 2 v 1, 2 v 1 becomes doubles). The court they left adjusts (doubles becomes 2 v 1, 2 v 1 becomes singles). With fewer than 12 players the singles match is on Court 3, so the half court (Court 4) is only used when you need it. Court 4 only takes singles, so it has no empty seats. A round you change is locked so Regenerate leaves it alone. Unlock it to let Regenerate redo it.
  • Clear All removes every round, locked ones too, after asking. Players and settings are kept. Reducing the number of rounds removes them from the end, and an Undo button appears for a moment.
  • Changing the roster, groups or settings after generating shows a notice. Regenerate to apply the change to unlocked rounds.

How the fixtures work

  • Rounds are built one at a time. The app tries to keep games evenly matched, to avoid repeat partners, opponents and foursomes (especially back-to-back), and to share singles, 2 v 1 and the other games fairly. The same settings always give the same fixtures.
  • Groups: group players by playing level, not friendship. Very small groups (one or two players) give the app little room to work with. For Social sessions, one group plus Beginners is usually enough: extra groups make only a small difference there, so it is simpler to keep the one group in a rough order. Groups matter most for Competitive sessions, where they keep each court closely matched. Keep the Beginner group in either style, because the beginner rules depend on it.
  • Ranking: the order within a group is a very rough ranking, strongest at the top. It only needs to be approximate: there is no need to get it perfect, and nobody should read anything into where they sit. It is only a gentle tie-break (for example, it helps decide who is the solo in a 2 v 1) and never overrides the groups or who plays how often. If you are unsure, leave it.
  • Competitive keeps players mostly with their own group, so games are closely matched. Social mixes neighbouring groups more freely, with a little built-in variety, so stronger and weaker players share a court more often; teams are still balanced (for example strong + weaker v strong + weaker). On small or lopsided rosters, or with few rounds, the two styles can give identical fixtures, and with only one group the style makes no difference.
  • Club sheets uses the club's own fixed fixtures for the number of active players, in roster order (slot 1 is the first, strongest player; groups and beginner rules are ignored, and No singles only produces a warning). It is only for a fresh session of exactly 5 rounds with 6 to 14 players. Once the rounds are generated the style switches to Social straight away (Regenerate rounds stays greyed out), so anything you change afterwards is rebuilt in the Social style around any locked rounds.
  • Beginners are kept together where possible and the repeat rules are relaxed for them. With two or more beginners, two of them play each other on the singles court in the first round and again around the middle of the session; with no singles court but a 2 v 1, two beginners are the pair in it. The app avoids putting the same strong players with beginners more than once, most of all the higher ranked Group 1 players. The Session summary shows a B games column (games played with a beginner on the court) when beginners are playing.
  • Hand edits: an edited round is locked, so Regenerate leaves it alone and rebuilds the other rounds around it.
